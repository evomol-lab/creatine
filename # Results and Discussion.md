# Results and Discussion

We have also identified the presence of Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF1), which serves as a pivotal connector among proteins, including enzymes and transporters, that are directly involved in creatine metabolism and uptake, as well as other proteins and transcription factors. IGF1 plays a significant role in muscle development and metabolism, and it is intricately associated with muscle mass, strength development, and the proliferative capacity of muscle satellite cells. It is primarily produced by liver hepatocytes stimulated by the growth hormone (Ahmad et al. 2020). Given the structural and functional similarities between IGF1/IGF1R and insulin receptor signaling, IGF1's influence extends beyond creatine metabolism to encompass broader metabolic functions (Kineman et al. 2018). IGF1 is also synthesized in multiple locations within the kidney, including mesangial cells, which are central to the development of kidney disease, with enhanced IGF1 signaling observed in various disease states, including diabetes (Bach et al. 2015). In addtion, altered IGF1 levels are linked to cardiovascular and renal diseases (Mohebi et al. 2023). This underscores the multifaceted role of IGF1 in both creatine handling and general metabolic processes.
