# Results and Discussion

We have also identified the presence of Insulin-like Growth Factor 1 (IGF1), which serves as a pivotal connector among proteins, including enzymes and transporters, that are directly involved in creatine metabolism and uptake, as well as other proteins and transcription factors. IGF1 plays a significant role in muscle development and metabolism, and it is intricately associated with muscle mass, strength development, and the proliferative capacity of muscle satellite cells[^1^][3][^2^][4]. It is primarily produced by liver hepatocytes and exerts its metabolic actions through extra-hepatocyte mechanisms[^3^][1]. Given the structural and functional similarities between IGF1/IGF1R and insulin receptor signaling, IGF1's influence extends beyond creatine metabolism to encompass broader metabolic functions. These include regulating hormonal inputs to the liver and altering tissue-specific carbohydrate and lipid metabolism, thereby integrating the metabolic needs of the entire organism[^3^][1]. This underscores the multifaceted role of IGF1 in both creatine handling and general metabolic processes.
