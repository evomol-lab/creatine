# 1. Introduction

Creatine (N-(aminoiminomethyl)-N-methylglycine) plays a pivotal role in cellular energy homeostasis by functioning as an essential component of the creatine/phosphocreatine (Cr/PCr) system. This spatial and temporal bioenergetic buffer maintains adenosine triphosphate (ATP) availability in tissues characterized by high and fluctuating metabolic demands, including skeletal and cardiac muscle, the renal parenchyma, liver, and the central nervous system [1, 2]. By facilitating rapid ADP rephosphorylation via creatine kinase isoforms, the Cr/PCr system buffers intracellular ATP concentrations, prevents metabolic acidosis, and regulates cellular oxidative stress and inflammatory signaling pathways [1, 3, 4].

The physiological architecture of creatine metabolism relies on a strictly regulated division of labor involving endogenous biosynthesis and cell-specific transport. Endogenous synthesis begins primarily in the kidneys and liver through a two-step enzymatic reaction [2, 3]. The first and rate-limiting step is catalyzed by glycine amidinotransferase, encoded by the **GATM** gene (UniProt P50440), which transfers the amidino group from L-arginine to glycine to form guanidinoacetate (GAA) [3]. Following GAA methylation to creatine by guanidinoacetate N-methyltransferase (GAMT), creatine is released into systemic circulation [2]. Because most target tissues—such as the brain and skeletal muscle—possess limited or no capacity for *de novo* synthesis, cellular uptake depends on the Na⁺/Cl⁻-dependent creatine transporter 1, encoded by the **SLC6A8** gene (UniProt P48029) [2, 3]. 

Given the fundamental importance of GATM and SLC6A8 in maintaining systemic metabolic equilibrium, understanding their evolutionary history provides critical insights into how energy distribution networks adapted across diverse animal lineages [2]. Recent transcriptomic and physiological studies have highlighted the tissue-specific expression of creatine-related genes under metabolic strain, such as renal pathologies, diabetes, and oxidative stress [1, 3, 4]. However, how evolutionary constraint and sequence divergence shaped GATM and SLC6A8 architecture from ancestral non-vertebrate lineage homologues to highly specialized vertebrate systems remains incompletely characterized.

To address this knowledge gap, the present study characterizes the evolutionary distribution, taxonomic diversification, and sequence conservation of **GATM** and **SLC6A8**. Using the automated phylogenetic workflow tool **TaxOnTree**, we performed multi-taxonomic BLAST searches and sequence alignments across varied sequence identity thresholds (50% and 75%) and retrieval capacities (200 and 800 sequences). By examining phylogenetic clustering patterns across distinct taxonomic depths, this work correlates sequence conservation with the functional specialization of creatine synthesis and transport across vertebrate tissues, providing a comprehensive framework for reconstructing the molecular evolution of the creatine metabolic system.
